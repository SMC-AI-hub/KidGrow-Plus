Handbook 2 — Breast milk: what makes it special

📖 Infant Feeding Handbook • English

Colostrum, transitional, and mature milk are living food — antibodies, brain-building fats, and gut-protecting sugars that no factory can copy.

WHAT TO DO AT HOME
  1. First milk (colostrum) is thick and yellow — the baby's first vaccine; every drop counts.
  2. Milk changes over days and even during a feed — let the baby finish one breast first.
  3. Antibodies and protective sugars guard against diarrhoea, pneumonia, and ear infections.
  4. Brain fats and growth factors support brain and eye development.
  5. Breastfed babies gain the right weight and get less obesity, diabetes, and allergies later.
  6. Mothers benefit too — faster recovery, delayed pregnancy, lower cancer risk.
AVOID THESE MISTAKES
  • Don't throw away colostrum or wait for 'white milk' — yellow milk is the most precious.
  • Don't switch breasts quickly — the later milk in a feed is richer in fat.
  • Don't believe 'thin milk' myths — foremilk looking watery is normal and healthy.
WHAT TO EXPECT
Milk looks bluish-thin at feed start and creamy-white later — both are normal. Breasts feeling softer after feeds means good flow, not low supply.
🚨 WHEN TO SEE YOUR DOCTOR
See the doctor: baby not latching by 24 hours, fewer than 6 wet nappies after day 4, weight loss over 10%, or mother with breast redness + fever + pain.
