Chapter 6 — Preterm & Low-Birth-Weight Feeding

Small babies lose heat and fluids fast, tire at the breast, and need more protein and minerals per kg than term babies — yet mother's own milk (MOM) remains their best feed, with donor milk next. This chapter maps the standard journey; exact unit protocols vary, and NICU staff decisions always lead.

6.4 Mother's milk for the small baby — practical points

  • Colostrum first: drops by spoon/syringe for oral care + first feeds from day 1 — immune protection the NICU cannot replicate.
  • Express early and often: within hours of birth, 8–12 times/24 h including night (see Ch.4). Early + frequent expression is the strongest predictor of later milk adequacy.
  • Non-nutritive sucking at the emptied breast during tube feeds builds coordination and supply.
  • Transition ladder: tube → cup/paladai/spoon → direct breast as rooting, latch, and stamina mature (often around 32–34 weeks, varies widely).
  • Donor milk: pasteurized donor human milk from a milk bank is the recommended fallback when MOM is insufficient — ask the unit; informal sharing is discouraged (§4.9).

6.6 Supplements & follow-up (doctor-led)

  • Iron, vitamin D, calcium–phosphorus, multivitamin: preterm babies need supplements per unit protocol — doses and timing are individualized; families should never start/stop on their own.
  • Follow-up is multi-system: weight/length/HC (corrected age, §9.7), eye (ROP screening on schedule — never miss), hearing, anaemia, bone health, neurodevelopment.
  • Immunize on chronological age (not corrected) per national schedule, unless the doctor defers a dose.

✅ Key messages — Chapter 6
  1. Immediate KMC, 8–24 h/day — it is treatment.
  2. Mother's milk from day 1: colostrum → early expression → enteral feeds → breast.
  3. Donor milk fills gaps; fortification is only for <32 wk / <1500 g per protocol.
  4. Supplements + ROP/hearing/growth follow-up are non-negotiable.
  5. Discharge with a written feeding + danger-sign plan; danger signs → same-day return.
👩‍⚕️ For the health workerCounsel the mother before discharge using teach-back: KMC hours, feed volumes/method, supplement doses, and three danger signs she can name. Link every discharged LBW baby to the ASHA/ANM for home weight checks in week 1.

Chapter 7 — Formula Feeding: Safe & Responsive

Some babies need formula — medically, or by informed family choice. This chapter is judgment-free and safety-first: which type (doctor decides), how to prepare it without making the baby sick, and how to bottle-feed responsively. Honesty rule: formula composition is regulated (FSSAI 2020); no brand claims or invented nutrient values appear here — always read the actual label.

7.1 When formula enters the picture

  • Rare medical reasons: e.g. classic galactosaemia, some maternal medicines/illnesses (doctor decides — see Ch.13 for the full list).
  • Informed choice: adoption, surrogacy, maternal separation without EBM access, or a mother's supported decision after lactation help.
  • Supplementation: temporary top-ups while fixing technique/supply — with a plan to return to exclusive breastfeeding where possible (see Ch.3–5 first).
⚠️ What formula cannot copy (see Ch.2)Antibodies, HMOs, live cells, enzymes, and adaptive composition. That is why every formula decision deserves skilled counselling first — and why mixed feeding should protect breastfeeding rather than replace it by default.

👩‍⚕️ Label-verification checklist (teach every formula family)
  1. Right product + age range? (Infant vs follow-up — they differ.)
  2. Exact water volume + exact level scoops? (Use ONLY the enclosed scoop.)
  3. Manufacturing + expiry dates, intact seal, correct storage after opening (usually cool/dry, use within ~4 weeks — read the pack)?
  4. Preparation water temperature and use-within time as printed?
  5. Batch number noted (in case of recall/illness)?

7.5 Everyday questions

QuestionAnswer
Spit-ups after every feed?Small possets with good weight gain are normal. Forceful vomiting, poor gain, blood, or distress → doctor.
Constipation?True constipation = hard dry pellets + straining. Formula stools are firmer than breastfed — check ratios first (too concentrated?). Don't switch formulas without advice.
Can we change brands?Any standard brand meeting regulations suits most babies; frequent switching for fussiness rarely helps. Suspected cow's-milk allergy (blood/mucus stools, eczema + gut symptoms, poor growth) needs doctor diagnosis before special formula.
Can family foods start early "because on formula"?No — complementary feeding still starts at 6 months (Ch.8).
👩‍⚕️ IMS Act reminder (India)Promotion of breast-milk substitutes to families is prohibited; health facilities must not display formula branding or accept company samples/gifts. Counsel objectively: support breastfeeding first, and teach safe formula use skilfully when it is genuinely needed — both are professional duties.
✅ Key messages — Chapter 7
  1. Doctor chooses the type; family verifies the label every tin.
  2. Powder isn't sterile: water ≥70 °C, exact level scoops, feed within 2 h.
  3. Batch-made bottles: chill fast, use within 24 h, warm gently.
  4. Upright baby, flat bottle, pauses, stop at cues — never prop.
  5. Allergy/special formulas only on diagnosis — never self-switch for fussiness.

Chapter 8 — Complementary Feeding 6–24 Months

At 6 months, breast milk alone no longer covers iron, zinc, and energy needs — but it stays the nutritional backbone till 2 years and beyond. "Complementary" means foods complement, not replace, milk. (WHO 2023 complementary-feeding guideline; Evidence Map rows E8–E10.)

8.1 When & why — readiness checklist

✔️ Start at 6 months (180 days) when baby:

  • Sits with support, holds head steady
  • Brings hands/objects to mouth
  • Shows interest in food, opens mouth for spoon
  • Tongue-thrust (pushing food out) fading

⏳ Wait / ask the doctor if:

  • Born preterm — usually ~6 months corrected age
  • Cannot sit supported or hold head up
  • Chokes/gags on every spoon (beyond normal learning gagging)
  • Never starts before 4 months; 6 months is the standard

8.2 The journey: textures, frequency, amounts

Fig 8.1 — Texture ladder + meal pattern 6–24 months. Energy gaps (≈200/300/550 kcal/day) are filled by foods; breast milk covers the rest. Amounts are guides — appetite varies daily; follow hunger/fullness cues (§8.4).

8.3 Building the bowl — the 4-part plate

A spoon of oil/ghee in the bowl — small stomachs need energy-dense food. 🩸 Iron-rich food daily — egg, meat/fish, ragi, green leaves + vitamin C (lemon, amla, guava, tomato) to absorb it. Tea with meals blocks iron — avoid. 🤱 Breast milk between meals — still ~⅓ to ½ of energy at 12–24 mo. 💧 Clean water in a cup with meals; no juices, colas, or packaged drinks. 🧂 Minimal salt/sugar — baby tastes; family adds at the table. 🥜 Nuts = ground/paste only (whole nuts choke). Introduce peanut/egg early — delaying does NOT prevent allergy (current guidance).

Fig 8.2 — The 4-part baby bowl + daily add-ons. Animal-source foods (egg, curd, fish, meat) most efficiently fill the iron/zinc gap; vegetarian families combine dals + grains + vit-C + oil and discuss B12/iron with the doctor.

8.4 Responsive feeding — how matters as much as what

  • Feed patiently, face-to-face: eye contact, talk/sing, follow the child's pace. No screens, no chasing with a spoon, no force.
  • Respect stop signals: turning away, pushing food, clamping mouth, playing — the meal ends. Forcing teaches food battles, not nutrition.
  • New foods need 8–10 tries: tiny tastes across days, not one rejected spoonful = "dislikes". Eat the same food yourself — modelling works.
  • Let them self-feed: fingers first, then spoon. Mess is motor learning; a mat under the chair saves the floor.
  • Hungry after illness? Offer extra meals/snacks for 2 weeks of recovery ("catch-up feeding") — appetite returns before weight does.

8.5 Hygiene — diarrhoea prevention is nutrition

  • Wash hands (cook + child) with soap before cooking/eating; clean utensils, safe water.
  • Cook fresh, serve promptly; store leftovers covered in a cool place and reheat bubbling-hot before serving. Discard what the child leaves in the bowl after 2 hours.
  • Keep animals/flies off food; cover water vessels.

8.7 Two sample days (examples, not prescriptions)

🌞 Sample day — 8 months

  • Morning breastfeed + ragi porridge with ghee + mashed banana
  • Midday: dal-rice-vegetable mash with oil + lemon
  • Evening snack: soft papaya fingers / curd
  • Night: khichdi mash + breastfeeds through day/night

🌞 Sample day — 14 months

  • Breakfast: family upma/idli pieces + egg + breastfeed
  • Lunch: rice + dal + greens + fish/chicken pieces + curd
  • Snack: banana + groundnut chikki (soft, supervised)
  • Dinner: family roti-sabzi (torn small) + breastfeed
✅ Key messages — Chapter 8
  1. Start at 6 months; breastfeed to 2 years and beyond.
  2. Thick foods from day 1; lumps by 8–9 months; family foods by 1 year.
  3. Every bowl: staple + protein + veg/fruit + oil; iron-rich food + vitamin C daily.
  4. Feed responsively: patient, face-to-face, stop at fullness, 8–10 tries for new foods.
  5. Honey and milk-as-drink wait till 1 year; juices and colas never.
👩‍⚕️ For the health workerDemonstrate consistency ("stays on the spoon") with a real bowl at the 6-month contact — watery gruels are the commonest failure. Ask to SEE a meal's thickness and the feeding interaction, not just the food list.

Chapter 9 — Growth Monitoring: Measure, Plot, Act

Growth is nutrition made visible. Regular measuring + correct plotting + prompt action on faltering prevents most malnutrition — this chapter makes every health worker and parent chart-literate.

9.1 What to measure, with what

MeasureToolTechnique essentials
WeightCalibrated infant/digital scaleZeroed; baby naked (or noted clothing); same scale each visit
Length (<2 yr)Length board, two peopleHead touching headboard, legs straight, feet flat — no "tape on bed"
Head circumferenceNon-stretch tapeWidest point (above brows, around occiput); track trend
MUAC (6–59 mo)MUAC tape, left armMidpoint shoulder–elbow; arm relaxed; read window precisely

9.2 Reading a WHO growth chart — the schematic

< −2 lines: underweight / stunted / wasted — act (§9.5) 📈 > +2 lines: overweight — review juices, snacks, portions ➡️ Parallel to a line: thriving, whatever the line ⚠️ Crossing 2 lines down: faltering even if still "normal" Plot weight-for-age, length-for-age, weight-for-length AND head circ.

9.5 Responding to faltering — assess, counsel, follow up

  1. Assess intake: 24-hour recall — breastfeeds (count incl. night), meal frequency, bowl contents (Fig 8.2), thickness, snacks, juices, who feeds, any force/refusal.
  2. Assess illness: recent diarrhoea/fever/cough, worms, TB contacts, recurrent infections; immunization status; maternal health and support.
  3. Counsel specifically: one or two changes (e.g. add egg + oil daily; thicken porridge; add a 4th meal) — written down, not a lecture.
  4. Follow up in 2–4 weeks: re-weigh same scale; improved trajectory = continue; flat/falling = refer for medical evaluation (anaemia, UTI, TB, allergy, developmental causes).

9.6 Preterm growth — corrected age + right charts

  • Use corrected age (age from due date) for plotting and milestones until 2–3 years.
  • In hospital: fetal/infant charts per unit (e.g. Fenton) until term; after term-equivalent age: WHO charts with corrected age.
  • Expect catch-up over months; judge the trajectory, and escalate flattening/falling exactly as in term babies.

9.7 The overweight side — don't miss it

  • Rising concern in cities: weight-for-length > +2 lines, rapid upward crossing.
  • Protective: exclusive breastfeeding 6 mo, continued to 2 yr, responsive feeding, no juices/SSBs, active floor play.
  • Counsel without shaming: swap sugary drinks for water/milk, shrink packaged snacks, protect sleep and play; re-plot in 1–2 months.
✅ Key messages — Chapter 9
  1. Measure right: zeroed scale, length board, two people, same tools.
  2. Plot all four indices on real WHO charts; read the line's direction.
  3. Parallel = thriving; flattening = review feeds; falling = act.
  4. MUAC: green routine, yellow counsel + 2-week review, red/oedema urgent referral.
  5. Preterm: corrected age to 2–3 years; WHO charts after term.
👩‍⚕️ For the health workerNever hand a mother a plotted card without explaining what the line means and agreeing ONE next step. An unplotted weight is a wasted visit; an unexplained plot is a wasted opportunity.
