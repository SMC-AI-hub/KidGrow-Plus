Complementary feeding (6–23 mo)

🩺 Everyday care • English

At 6 months (180 days) breastmilk alone can't meet iron, zinc and energy needs — family foods begin, while breastfeeding continues to 2 years. Food before 6 months harms; after 6 months, delay harms.

🥄 Golden rule: thick enough to stay on the spoon, colourful enough to cover 4+ food groups a day.
WHAT TO DO AT HOME
  1. Start at 6 months: thick porridge/mash that stays on the spoon — thin gruel fills the stomach with water, not nutrition.
  2. Build every meal: staple (rice/ragi/wheat) + dal/egg/meat + vegetable/fruit + a spoon of oil/ghee.
  3. Animal-source foods daily — egg, fish, chicken or meat; fruits + vegetables daily; groundnut/til for energy.
  4. Increase texture with age: mash (6–8) → chopped + finger foods (9–11) → family foods (12+).
  5. Wash hands + utensils; cook fresh, serve warm; no bottles — use katori-spoon.
  6. During illness feed more often (soft favourites + extra breastfeeds); after illness give one extra meal daily for 2 weeks.
  7. Weigh monthly — the growth curve tells you feeding is working before eyes can.

AVOID THESE MISTAKES
  • Don't force-feed or distract-feed with a phone/TV — it spoils hunger cues and bonding.
  • Don't give honey, cow's milk as a main drink, tea/coffee, cola, or packaged juices in year one — and no added salt/sugar before 1 year.
  • Don't delay textures past 9 months — late chewing slows speech muscles too.
WHAT TO EXPECT
New foods may be refused 8–10 times before acceptance — offer, don't push. Appetite dips around 12–15 months — small frequent meals win. Non-breastfed babies need 4–5 small meals a day.
🚨 WHEN TO SEE YOUR DOCTOR
Discuss with doctor: poor weight gain, frequent vomiting, blood in stool, food refusal >2 weeks.
