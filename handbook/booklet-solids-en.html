Complementary feeding (6–23 mo)

🩺 Everyday care • English
© 2026 KidGrow Pro • All rights reserved

At 6 months (180 days) breastmilk alone can't meet iron, zinc and energy needs — family foods begin, while breastfeeding continues to 2 years. Food before 6 months harms; after 6 months, delay harms.

🥄 Golden rule: thick enough to stay on the spoon, colourful enough to cover 4+ food groups a day.
WHAT TO DO AT HOME
  1. Start at 6 months: thick porridge/mash that stays on the spoon — thin gruel fills the stomach with water, not nutrition.
  2. Build every meal: staple (rice/ragi/wheat) + dal/egg/meat + vegetable/fruit + a spoon of oil/ghee.
  3. Animal-source foods daily — egg, fish, chicken or meat; fruits + vegetables daily; groundnut/til for energy.
  4. Increase texture with age: mash (6–8) → chopped + finger foods (9–11) → family foods (12+).
  5. Wash hands + utensils; cook fresh, serve warm; no bottles — use katori-spoon.
  6. During illness feed more often (soft favourites + extra breastfeeds); after illness give one extra meal daily for 2 weeks.
  7. Weigh monthly — the growth curve tells you feeding is working before eyes can.

EASY, LOW-COST RECIPES
6m+

Ragi Porridge (Ragi Kanji)

Ingredients: 2 tbsp ragi flour, 1 cup water or milk, a little jaggery/mashed banana

Method: Mix flour with a little water to a smooth paste. Stir into boiling water/milk. Cook 5–7 min until thick. Cool and sweeten lightly.

Approx. cost: Rs. 3–4 per serving  |  Approx. energy: ~120–150 kcal/serving

8m+

Moong Dal Khichdi

Ingredients: 2 tbsp rice, 1 tbsp moong dal, chopped carrot/pumpkin, 1/4 tsp ghee

Method: Wash rice and dal. Pressure cook with vegetables and water (1:4) until very soft. Mash well, add ghee.

Approx. cost: Rs. 5–6 per serving  |  Approx. energy: ~150 kcal/half katori

8m+

Vegetable Dalia

Ingredients: 2 tbsp broken wheat (dalia), mixed chopped vegetables, 1/4 tsp oil/ghee

Method: Dry-roast dalia lightly. Pressure cook with vegetables and water until soft and mushy. Mash well.

Approx. cost: Rs. 4–5 per serving  |  Approx. energy: ~130 kcal/serving

7m+

Dal-Vegetable Mash

Ingredients: 2 tbsp toor/masoor dal, carrot or pumpkin, 1/4 tsp ghee

Method: Cook dal and vegetable until soft. Mash thoroughly to a smooth, thick consistency. Add ghee.

Approx. cost: Rs. 4–5 per serving  |  Approx. energy: ~140–160 kcal/serving

9m+

Idli with Mashed Sambar

Ingredients: 1 soft idli, 2–3 tbsp thin mild vegetable sambar

Method: Mash a soft idli with a little mild sambar until smooth enough to swallow easily.

Approx. cost: Rs. 5–6 per serving  |  Approx. energy: ~150 kcal/serving

6m+

Egg Mash

Ingredients: 1 boiled egg (yolk first, then whole), a splash of breast milk/water

Method: Hard-boil the egg. Fork-mash the yolk (later the white too), loosening with milk or water.

Approx. cost: Rs. 6–8 per serving  |  Approx. energy: ~78 kcal/egg

8m+

Chicken-Vegetable Mash

Ingredients: 2 tbsp boneless chicken, carrot/pumpkin, 1/4 tsp ghee

Method: Boil chicken and vegetable until very tender. Shred finely, mash together, add ghee.

Approx. cost: Rs. 8–10 per serving  |  Approx. energy: ~120–140 kcal/serving

Costs are rough local estimates and vary by region and season. Millets, seasonal vegetables, and local fruits are the most economical daily choices.

MYTHS VS FACTS
MYTHFACT
Starting solids before 6 months helps baby sleep betterNo evidence; early solids raise infection and allergy risk
Rice water or thin gruel alone is enoughToo watery — baby needs thick, energy-dense food
Non-veg food is unsafe for babiesWell-cooked egg, chicken, fish are excellent iron + protein
Give food only when baby cries for itOffer actively and regularly; don't wait for hunger cues
AVOID THESE MISTAKES
  • Don't force-feed or distract-feed with a phone/TV — it spoils hunger cues and bonding.
  • Don't give honey, cow's milk as a main drink, tea/coffee, cola, or packaged juices in year one — and no added salt/sugar before 1 year.
  • Don't delay textures past 9 months — late chewing slows speech muscles too.
WHAT TO EXPECT
New foods may be refused 8–10 times before acceptance — offer, don't push. Appetite dips around 12–15 months — small frequent meals win. Non-breastfed babies need 4–5 small meals a day.
🚨 WHEN TO SEE YOUR DOCTOR
Discuss with doctor: poor weight gain, frequent vomiting, blood in stool, food refusal >2 weeks.
