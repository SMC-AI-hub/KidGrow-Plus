Chapter 10 — The Mother: Nutrition, Rest & Mood

A well-fed, rested, supported mother breastfeeds longer and enjoys it more. This chapter covers her extra needs, busts food myths, and flags when low mood needs care — because maternal health is infant feeding.

10.1 Extra needs while breastfeeding

Fig 10.1 — Maternal extra needs. Approximate additions over her usual diet; thin mothers, adolescents, and mothers of twins need individual assessment. Supplements continue per programme/doctor — diet plus tablets, not tablets instead of diet.

10.2 Food myths — gentle corrections

MythTruth
"Eat only ghee laddoos / special foods"Festive foods are fine in moderation, but a diverse normal diet beats any single "milk food". No single food makes milk — frequent feeding does.
"Avoid 'cold' foods / spicy foods"No evidence; eat what the family eats. Watch the baby, not the food list.
"Drink milk to make milk"Fluids to thirst suffice; cow's milk adds calcium/protein but isn't mandatory.
"You'll get fat / stay thin because of breastfeeding"Weight change varies; gradual return with normal diet + activity. Crash dieting can sap energy and supply — eat adequately.

10.3 Rest, help & mood — the support wheel

Blues (days 3–10): tearful, overwhelmed, but Bonding OK and moments of joy return. Needs rest + support, resolves <2 weeks. 🚨 Needs SAME-WEEK care if ≥2 weeks: • Persistent sadness/emptiness, crying most days • Can't bond / resentful toward baby; severe anxiety, panic • No appetite/sleep even when baby sleeps; can't function • Hopelessness, guilt, or ANY thoughts of harm → urgent care Depression is illness, not failure — treatment works and most medicines are breastfeeding-compatible (doctor confirms, §12.4).

Fig 10.2 — Support wheel + mood red flags. Ask every mother "how are YOU?" at each contact — and ask in private at least once. Fathers/families: practical help (cooking, night shifts, older children) protects breastfeeding more than advice does.

10.4 Birth spacing & contraception while breastfeeding

  • LAM (lactational amenorrhoea) protects only if ALL three hold: baby <6 months + exclusively breastfed day & night + mother's periods not returned. If any fails → use another method.
  • Compatible options: copper-IUD, condoms, progestin-only pills/injectable (usually after 6 weeks), sterilization when family complete. Combined pills are generally deferred while breastfeeding — doctor advises.
  • Plan before the 6-week visit — fertility can return before the first period.
✅ Key messages — Chapter 10
  1. Add a meal + snack daily; diverse family food; fluids to thirst.
  2. Continue IFA + calcium; postpartum deworming per programme.
  3. No food restrictions without a clear, repeated baby reaction.
  4. Sleep, shared chores, company, movement — prescribe support like medicine.
  5. Low mood ≥2 weeks or any harm thoughts → care this week, urgently if severe.
👩‍⚕️ For the health workerWeigh the mother, check pallor, and ask about mood at postnatal contacts — she is your second patient. Link anaemia, depression, and domestic-stress cases to care; do not counsel and forget.

Chapter 11 — Feeding During Illness

Illness is when good feeding matters most — and when families most often stop it. Rule of the chapter: feed through illness, feed extra in recovery.

11.1 The sick baby — feed more, not less

  • Breastfeed more often, shorter feeds — a sick baby tires quickly; small frequent feeds + skin-to-skin keep intake up.
  • Keep complementary foods going if the child accepts — soft, favourite, small portions. Never starve a diarrhoea ("rest the gut" is harmful).
  • Diarrhoea: ORS after every loose stool in addition to breast milk/feeds + zinc for 14 days (dose per doctor/programme). Continue formula at normal strength — never dilute.
  • Fever/cold/cough: continue feeds + fluids; clear a blocked nose (saline drops, upright position) before breastfeeds; lukewarm sponging for comfort; fever medicine only as the doctor directs.
  • Vomiting: pause 10–15 min, then small frequent sips/feeds; ORS by spoon. Red flags: green/bloody vomit, projectile vomiting, no urine 6+ h, lethargy → urgent care.
  • After illness: 2 weeks of catch-up — an extra meal/snack daily until weight trajectory recovers (§9.5).

11.2 Dehydration check + care flowchart

Fig 11.1 — Sick-child feeding + dehydration triage. Danger signs bypass home care entirely. Note: fast breathing, chest indrawing, stridor, or fever in a baby <2 months are also same-day/urgent triggers even without diarrhoea.

11.3 Medicines — what helps, what harms

MedicineRoleCaution
ORSReplaces water + salts in diarrhoea/vomitingCorrect dilution (1 sachet : 1 litre safe water); use within 24 h
Zinc (14 days)Shortens diarrhoea, prevents recurrenceDose per doctor/programme; complete the course
Prescribed antibioticsOnly for diagnosed bacterial illnessFull course, correct dose; never self-start or share
ParacetamolFever comfort per doctor's weight-based doseMeasure with syringe, never guess; no aspirin in children
Cough syrups, anti-diarrhoealsNot recommended in young childrenCan harm; honey (only >1 yr) + fluids soothe coughs better

11.4 Hospital stays — protect breastfeeding

  • Mother stays with the baby; KMC and breastfeeding continue unless the treating team orders otherwise.
  • If separation is unavoidable: express 8–12×/day (Ch.4), cup-feed EBM, and get relactation support before discharge (§12.8).
  • Take the growth card + feeding history to every admission — "what and how the child eats" is part of the diagnosis.
✅ Key messages — Chapter 11
  1. Feed through illness: more frequent breastfeeds + ORS + continued foods.
  2. ORS + zinc for diarrhoea; normal-strength formula; never starve or dilute.
  3. Danger signs → emergency; some dehydration → clinic today; mild → home care + watch.
  4. No self-medication with antibiotics/cough syrups; finish prescribed courses.
  5. Catch-up feeding for 2 weeks after every illness.

Chapter 12 — Special Situations

Most "reasons to stop breastfeeding" are myths. This chapter gives the evidence-based line for each special situation — and the honesty to say where the doctor must individualize.

12.1 The master rule — continue, pause, or stop?

Fig 12.1 — Continue / pause / stop matrix. The green list surprises families — lead with it. Every amber/red decision is made WITH the treating doctor and ART/TB/nuclear-medicine teams, never by the family alone.

12.2 HIV — breastfeed with ART, exclusively first

  • Mothers on effective ART with suppressed viral load should exclusively breastfeed 6 months, then continue to 24 months with complementary foods (national/WHO guidance for our setting).
  • Exclusive beats mixed: mixed feeding in the first 6 months carries higher transmission risk than exclusive breastfeeding — no water, teas, or top-ups.
  • Baby receives ARV prophylaxis + early testing per the ART programme; adherence (mother + baby) is the protection — link, don't just advise.

12.3 TB, hepatitis, and other infections

  • TB: breast milk doesn't transmit TB. Mother wears a mask and practices cough hygiene in the early infectious phase; baby is evaluated for preventive therapy; treatment of the mother is the baby's best protection.
  • Hepatitis B: birth-dose vaccine (± immunoglobulin per protocol) then breastfeed normally.
  • Hepatitis C: breastfeed normally; pause the affected side only if nipples are cracked and bleeding (express and discard until healed).

12.4 Maternal medicines — the practical script

  1. "Tell every prescriber you're breastfeeding" — most drugs have compatible alternatives or timing strategies.
  2. Don't stop chronic medicines (epilepsy, thyroid, BP, diabetes, mental health) from fear — uncontrolled disease harms mother and baby more than the milk dose of most drugs.
  3. Refer, don't guess: chemotherapy, psychiatric combinations, new/rare drugs → doctor/pharmacist checks compatibility resources; the health worker follows up the answer.

12.5 Alcohol, tobacco & other substances

SubstanceGuidance
AlcoholBest avoided. If any: rare, small, single amount — breastfeed FIRST, then wait ~2–3 hours per standard drink before the next feed; never bed-share after drinking.
Tobacco / gutka / betelQuit with help; breastfeeding still preferred over formula meanwhile. Smoke/chew away from the baby, change outer clothes, wash hands/face before handling.
Sedatives / opioids / recreational drugsDoctor-supervised plan — some prescribed substitution treatments allow breastfeeding; illicit use needs de-addiction linkage, not just feeding advice.

12.6 Cleft lip / palate — feed upright, get the team

  • Cleft lip alone: many babies breastfeed with a wide latch + cheek support — try with guidance.
  • Cleft palate: can't generate suction — feed upright with expressed milk via soft squeezable bottle/cup/paladai; specialist feeding plate/surgery planning via the cleft team.
  • Weigh frequently; poor gain → urgent feeding review. Surgery timing doesn't wait for "chubbiness" — the team balances both.

12.7 Low tone / Down syndrome / neurological conditions

  • Upright, well-supported positioning; chin-and-jaw support (health worker demonstrates); small, frequent, patient feeds.
  • Watch for unsafe swallow (coughing/choking every feed, wet voice, chest infections) → doctor + speech-therapy referral; short-term tube feeding may bridge.
  • Developmental therapy + feeding therapy together; growth on standard charts with clinical context.

12.8 Relactation & induced lactation — milk can return

  1. Frequent stimulation: baby to breast 8–12×/day + expression after; skin-to-skin; let the baby sleep near (safely).
  2. Bridge feeds: supplement at the breast (tube device) or by cup so the baby associates breast with fullness; reduce supplements as supply rises.
  3. Medicines (e.g. domperidone) only on prescription with cardiac screening — never over-the-counter on a neighbour's advice.
  4. Realistic hope: drops in days, partial supply in 1–2 weeks, full in weeks — any breast milk gained helps; success needs support, not just technique.

12.9 Emergencies — protect breastfeeding first

  • Stress does NOT dry up milk; separation and unsolicited formula do. Keep pairs together, feed on cue, create private feeding spaces.
  • No untargeted formula distribution — supplies go only to assessed non-breastfed infants with preparation support + safe water (IYCF-in-emergencies principle; IMS Act applies in crises too).
  • Prioritize: safe water, ready-to-eat food for mothers, relactation tents, growth screening.
✅ Key messages — Chapter 12
  1. Nearly all maternal illness and medicines allow continued breastfeeding — check, don't assume.
  2. HIV + ART: exclusive 6 months → 24 months; adherence is protection.
  3. Cleft/low-tone babies: upright, supported, EBM bridges, specialist teams.
  4. Relactation works with stimulation + bridging + time + support.
  5. In emergencies, protect breastfeeding pairs first; formula only by assessment.

Chapter 13 — Counselling Skills for Health Workers

Knowledge in this handbook only works if families adopt it. Counselling — listening, praising, suggesting, and checking — is the delivery system for all 12 previous chapters.

13.1 GATHER — six steps, every contact

Fig 13.1 — GATHER counselling flow. Works for clinic, home visit, or phone follow-up. The two most-skipped steps — praise-first (T) and teach-back (E) — are the two that change behaviour.

13.2 Words that open vs words that close

Instead of… ❌Say… ✅
"Why aren't you breastfeeding?""Tell me how feeding has been going."
"Your milk is not enough.""Let's check weight and feeds together and make a plan."
"You must / you should…""What would help you try…? What gets in the way?"
"That's wrong.""Many families do that — here's what we now know works better…"
Talking only to the mother while grandmother decides"Grandmother's experience matters — let's decide together what's best now."

13.3 The home-visit checklist (15–20 minutes)

  1. Greet + ask (open questions, private moment with mother).
  2. Watch a feed (position, latch, swallowing, mother's comfort).
  3. Weigh + plot on the card; explain the line in one sentence.
  4. Check: breasts, output (wets/stools), cord/jaundice (newborn), mother's rest/mood/pallor.
  5. Praise + 1–2 agreed actions + danger signs (teach-back).
  6. Write on the card; fix return date; record in register.

13.5 Referral triggers — the master list

🚨 Refer SAME DAY / urgently: any newborn danger sign (won't feed, lethargic, convulsions, fast breathing, fever/hypothermia) • severe dehydration • blood in stool/vomit + unwell child • SAM (MUAC <11.5/oedema) • cracked bleeding nipples with no improvement • mastitis not improving in 24 h on treatment • maternal depression with harm thoughts • suspected TB/HIV needing programme linkage.
⚠️ Refer routinely (non-urgent): flattening/falling growth despite 2 counselled reviews • persistent food refusal • suspected cow's-milk allergy • cleft/low-tone feeding difficulty • relactation needing medication assessment • maternal medicine-compatibility questions • overweight with rapid upward crossing.
✅ Key messages — Chapter 13
  1. GATHER every contact: greet, ask, tell (praise first), help, explain (teach-back), return date.
  2. Open questions, no judging words, include the grandmother.
  3. Every visit: watch a feed + weigh + plot + explain + agree 1–2 actions.
  4. Know the contact schedule and the two referral lists by heart.
  5. Record everything — the card speaks when you can't be there.
